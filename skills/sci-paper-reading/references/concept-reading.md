# Familiar-topic concept reading

Load for innovation-focused reading of a known field. The author's target is a useful new way of thinking, not an inventory of modules. Apply the kernel once; do not load onboarding unless a genuinely unfamiliar prerequisite prevents understanding.

## Read selectively, judge against content

First inspect the title/abstract, actual contribution passages, key method figure and conclusions to locate the claim. Then read the method sections/formulas and experiments necessary to verify that claim. Skip conventional implementation detail in the explanation unless it changes the principle or the user's requested reproduction. Do not confuse selective reading with reading only an abstract and calling the novelty verified.

Ask, using actual source evidence:

1. What specific view/assumption/problem formulation changes? Translate it into ordinary Chinese without author-invented module names.
2. What does the closest inspected prior approach assume/do, and what precisely differs? Separate a new concept, a known concept transferred to new conditions, a meaningful engineering realization and a routine recombination. Multiple categories can coexist; unknown prior art stays unverified.
3. Why is the mechanism a consequence of that idea rather than just another diagram? Explain the link from assumption/problem to operation, not a sequence of boxes.
4. Which comparison distinguishes the proposed explanation from a simpler alternative? An overall score increase does not establish the concept's necessity. Check targeted controls/ablations, qualitative evidence, theory or failure conditions proportionately; missing discrimination is a gap, not proof the idea is false.
5. What can transfer to the author's project, under which prerequisites, and what would have to be tested? Inspiration is not an empirical result, experiment plan approval or paper-writing authorization.

The counterfactual “without this principle, what problem remains?” tests the paper's own argument. It must not become “all other designs are necessarily wrong.” Do not upgrade a familiar loss/graph/module to conceptual innovation simply because it has a memorable name. Conversely a simple architecture can encode a substantial change of perspective. Absence of a large concept does not imply poor scientific quality or an invalid application contribution.

## Default brief response

For one paper, normally use about 300–600 Chinese characters, adjustable to the request:

- One sentence stating the core idea.
- The material difference from its closest inspected alternative, or a clear novelty-verification gap.
- The one or two decisive source/evidence locators and their boundary.
- What is reusable, and whether to read deeply, skim selected passages or set aside for this author's current purpose.

For a routine structural variant, a short reason and source may be enough. Do not generate the full template when there is little relevant novelty. For multiple papers compare concept/assumption/evidence/transfer in one table rather than repeat complete summaries. Recommended reading priority is subjective to the author's goal, not a universal paper-quality rating.

## Explicit deeper questions

Expand only the requested target. For equations give the real variable meanings, inputs/outputs, assumptions and how the expression realizes the principle; preserve symbols and inspect garbled source visually. For a figure, distinguish mechanism diagram from empirical evidence. For a result, state the actual task, metric, comparator and conditions; do not infer significance, causality or robustness from a table alone. A request for code reproduction, full formal critique or new experiments switches to that narrow skill, rather than silently expanding every reading.

If saving is authorized, record identity/version, reading scope, core idea, novelty basis/closest sources, decisive locators, boundary and transfer hypothesis in the one existing reading note. Preserve user annotations and label model interpretation; do not turn every paragraph into a second database.
