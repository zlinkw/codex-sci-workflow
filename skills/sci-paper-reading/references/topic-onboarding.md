# New-topic onboarding

Load only for a topic the author wants to learn, not every newly published paper. Apply the reading kernel once. Reuse already-known concepts and the stated research direction; ask only for a missing task boundary that materially changes the reading path.

## Build the problem before the bibliography

Explain the concrete situation that makes the topic necessary, the task/input/output and evaluation conditions, then just enough background to understand the difficulty. Distinguish nearby settings that change assumptions, such as supervised adaptation, UDA, source-free adaptation and test-time adaptation; use the actual topic's distinctions rather than always importing these examples.

Organize a compact landscape by competing ideas/problem assumptions, not a chronological list of paper abstracts. For each route explain:

- Which obstacle it addresses and why the obstacle arises.
- The core assumption or principle it uses.
- What that enables and what remains under compatible conditions.
- Which original/representative paper supplies the evidence.

Challenges may be conceptual, evidential or deployment-related. Mark established limitations versus the reader's hypotheses. Do not manufacture a challenge for each module or transfer the writing skill's Introduction structure into a field tutorial mechanically.

## Choose a useful small reading path

An MVP starts from roughly 5–8 well-chosen papers when enough suitable sources are available; this is a soft workload budget, not a coverage or classic-paper quota. Prefer these roles as appropriate:

- Original formulation/mechanism: where the idea actually originated, with version/date verified.
- Turning point: why a later framing changed how the problem was approached.
- Strong representative/current baseline: what is used for a meaningful modern comparison.
- A different or contradictory route: which assumption it questions or boundary it exposes.

Older, highly cited or prestigious does not alone mean foundational. A survey helps navigate but does not replace the original mechanism. Trace key references backward and selected follow-ups forward using the literature skill only for actual discovery. Avoid uncontrolled graph expansion and repeated variants of the same idea. Label candidates not yet inspected, and do not claim exhaustive or systematic coverage from a small map.

For each recommended paper give verified identity/link, its role, one reason to read, the relevant section/figure and any missing access. Order by prerequisite relationships, not necessarily year. End with the main unresolved question and an explicit next paper/reason.

## Default presentation

Give a readable problem/challenge/idea overview, one compact route table and the small reading order. Around 800–1200 Chinese characters plus the table is a starting budget; reduce/expand to the actual task. No standard textbook preamble or exhaustive acronym glossary. Explain unfamiliar terms at first use and expand only the prerequisites the author needs. Any conceptual map follows the typed/evidence-bearing relationship rules; citation lines do not certify the historical origin of an idea.
