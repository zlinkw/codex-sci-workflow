## Title, acronym and keyword rules

The author's default is **常见英文单词式缩写：具体描述**. Apply this preference to paper-title work automatically; it is not a journal-wide mandate or permission to rename an existing method during an unrelated edit. Use the confirmed problem and innovation, actual method and supported scope before choosing the name. Current verified venue requirements and the author's project decisions take priority.

### Core logic and acronym selection

Use **核心问题 → 现有处理的具体不足 → 本文改变了什么 → 能概括这种改变的名称** as a selection check, not text to fit into the title. The rendered title normally keeps **［有含义的方法名］: ［最具区分度的机制或原则］ for ［具体任务及必要条件］**. Other natural constructions are welcome. The description after the colon must identify the work even for someone unfamiliar with the acronym; emphasize one coherent core innovation rather than list modules or narrate the motivation.

- Prefer a familiar, pronounceable English word, usually **4–7 letters**, whose meaning evokes the core problem, object or solution principle. This is a soft preference, not a fixed count or license for an inflated semantic promise.
- Prefer ordered initials from meaningful words in a natural expansion. Function words and nonessential qualifiers may remain outside the acronym; not every title word needs a letter. Keep letter provenance clear. Scientific accuracy and natural English outrank semantic wordplay and perfect letter coverage; offer alternatives instead of inventing mechanisms, awkward synonyms or filler to force a word.
- Keep the method name, expansion and scientific meaning consistent across title, abstract, introduction, method, figures and reply. Define names where the actual template requires; a title acronym is not a substitute for required definitions. Preserve the author's chosen existing name unless renaming is in scope and approved.
- Before final selection, search the acronym with the relevant research area, expanded phrase and candidate title across appropriate scholarly sources and code repositories. Record the checked scope and confusing nearest names. A common word may be reused elsewhere; do not promise global uniqueness or automatically rename an established author method. Unchecked names remain provisional.

### Title templates, length and claim boundaries

| 适用重点 | 中文逻辑模板 |
|---|---|
| 特色机制 | ［名称］：［关键机制］用于［具体任务］ |
| 判断或优化原则的改变 | ［名称］：［核心原则］驱动的［任务或学习过程］ |
| 研究条件决定方法价值 | ［名称］：［关键创新］用于［明确条件下的任务］ |

Usually target **12–18 English words**; **10–15** is welcome when complete and natural. Prefer no more than **20**, as a soft ceiling rather than a reason to omit a consequential supervision, source-access or modality condition. Count readable title words including the method name, excluding TeX markup; record the counting convention for compounds if material. These are personal defaults, not measured TMI/MedIA norms or verified publisher limits. Use one colon by default and avoid additional subtitle/parenthesis layers. The intentional naming colon is compatible with the general rule against unnecessary prose colons.

Keep only the whole-method acronym by default, not a chain of module acronyms. Prefer specific scientific content over `novel`, `effective`, `powerful` or `state-of-the-art`. Include `robust`, `generalizable`, `interpretable` or `efficient` only when substantively defined, central and supported by corresponding evidence. Retain conditions such as unsupervised or source-free when dropping them changes the problem. A popular backbone belongs in the title only if it is genuinely central to the contribution. Apply the existing evidence and style rules, preserve official spellings, and follow the verified target template's title casing rather than importing cited-reference casing rules.

Evaluate a candidate name using the current study's actual contribution: **［名称的词义］ ↔ ［真实研究问题或关键原则］**, with **［自然展开中的有效词语］ → ［字母来源］**. Fill these slots from the current manuscript rather than reusing another paper's acronym, expansion, task or mechanism. A well-formed hypothetical name is not a verified unused name or evidence of an implemented method.

### Complementary, searchable keywords

Default to **4–6 keywords or short phrases**, selected by **任务＋学习设定＋核心机制＋关键科学概念＋必要应用限定**. These are coverage dimensions, not five compulsory slots. Prefer stable field terms, with modality, organ or disease included only when consequential. Follow current verified journal counts, controlled vocabulary, spelling, order and template requirements; do not infer hard limits from another skill or a published example.

- Title overlap is allowed and often useful for retrieval. Avoid redundant keyword slots, not necessary shared terms: do not list an acronym and its expansion separately, equivalent synonyms, or several broad umbrella terms where a specific task term suffices.
- Do not default to the new method acronym as a keyword or enumerate all named modules. Choose concepts readers would search for; do not inflate novelty by inventing keyword terminology.
- Derive candidate keywords from the current study's task, setting, mechanism, scientific concepts and consequential application conditions. Do not inherit another paper's keyword set or assume that every coverage dimension needs a separate slot.
- Distinguish printed Index Terms/Keywords from submission-system classification terms and database-assigned indexing terms. Their purpose, number and vocabulary need not match. In particular, TMI submission-system category selections do not establish the printed Index Terms count.

### Author selection and header persistence

When asked to propose a title, normally offer **three candidates**, each with a Chinese interpretation, English word count, acronym expansion and letter mapping, emphasized innovation, necessary scope qualifiers, collision-search status and any unsupported wording to avoid. Reuse a confirmed innovation framing rather than reopening whole-paper logic unnecessarily. Obtain the author's choice before replacing an established title/name, then check affected terminology and keywords within the authorized scope.

Use the main top profile's title/acronym/keyword fields for the chosen name, natural letter mapping, necessary qualifiers, keyword coverage and collision/venue verification. The global contract and source-comment ownership rules govern placement, scope and in-place updates; do not duplicate the entire candidate discussion.
