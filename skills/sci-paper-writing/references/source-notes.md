## Manuscript voice and grammatical subjects

Checked 2026-11-04. The author's preference is to avoid unnecessary first person when a clear process, module, model or other existing noun accurately serves as subject; first person remains allowed when clearer or more natural. The kernel owns this preference, which is neither a pronoun ban nor a requirement for uniformly passive writing. Oxford's primary-source search excerpts were available, but direct opening returned HTTP 403; MIT's methods guide opened successfully.

- [Oxford MPLS, The passive and active voices and when to use them](https://www.mpls.ox.ac.uk/training/resources-for-researcher-and-career-development/communication-skills/scientific-writing/the-passive-and-active-voices-and-when-to-to-use-them) distinguishes active voice from personal pronouns: an operation can be the subject of an active sentence. It also recognizes accepted first-person usage and the relevance of supervisor and journal preferences.
- [MIT NSE Communication Lab, Journal Article: Methods, section 2.3](https://mitcommlab.mit.edu/nse/commkit/journal-article-methods/) discusses both voices, including active clauses whose subject is not the experimenter, and advises following the target journal and research group's style.
- [Nature Portfolio, How to write your paper](https://www.nature.com/nature-portfolio/for-authors/write) recommends clear, direct sentences and active voice, with a first-person example. Its indexed primary-source text was inspected; direct opening redirected to an inaccessible login service. It supports clarity, not a prohibition of we. No IEEE/TMI-wide ban was established by this check.

Illustrative optional rephrasings, not mandatory replacements or claims about a real project. Use the manuscript's established model/module wording when needed; preserve the actual tense and facts. Keep the first-person version when it better identifies a researcher action or reads more naturally in context.

| First-person wording | Impersonal wording | Subject choice |
|---|---|---|
| We use the module to estimate the weights. | The module estimates the weights. | Actual computational actor |
| We evaluated the model on two datasets. | The model was evaluated on two datasets. | Evaluated object, without assigning evaluation to the model |
| Our results indicate a difference. | The results indicate a difference. | Evidence, with the same claim strength |
| We denote the weight by $w$. | The weight is denoted by $w$. | Defined object, preserving notation |

## Response-writing provenance

- [PLOS response guidance](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1005730): full subquestion coverage, direct answers, self-contained replies and original/revised distinctions.
- [Frontiers revision guidance](https://www.frontiersin.org/for-authors/submitting-research/how-to-respond-reviewer-comments): substantive changes before cosmetics and manuscript clarification after misunderstanding.
- [Freshwater Science editorial](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/688886): reasoned disagreement and conflicting requests. Shared-fix grouping, persistent TeX state and per-round navigation adapt the guidance to this author's workflow.
- [Academic Research Skills revision coach](https://github.com/Imbad0202/academic-research-skills/blob/main/academic-paper/agents/revision_coach_agent.md), [patch protocol](https://github.com/Imbad0202/academic-research-skills/blob/main/academic-paper/references/revision_patch_protocol.md): separate issue dimensions, commitment checks and exact-scope edits. Adapted without its multi-agent or sidecar-file pipeline.
- [TobiasLee Rebuttal-Skill](https://github.com/TobiasLee/Rebuttal-Skill/blob/main/SKILL.md): direct answer → evidence → revision, uncertain reviewer-intent diagnosis and information-value-driven experiment selection. Adapt without acceptance predictions, automatic experiments, conference-score strategy, cross-reviewer regrouping that breaks journal format or future-tense promises presented as completed journal revisions.
- [K-Dense scientific writing](https://github.com/K-Dense-AI/scientific-agent-skills/blob/main/skills/scientific-writing/SKILL.md): preserve scientific qualifiers and separate mechanical checks from evidence verification and final approval.
- [TMI author instructions](https://ieeetmi.org/authors-instructions/), revision/resubmission sections: point-by-point responses; the invited-resubmission section places each original comment immediately before its response. Follow the current actual decision letter and template rather than treating every submission type identically.
- [Elsevier peer review and resubmission](https://www-prod.elsevier.com/publishing/publish-in-a-journal/peer-review-and-resubmission): focused complete replies and respectful evidence-based disagreement. These are publisher-level recommendations, not verified MedIA-specific sentence/count rules; the [MedIA author guide](https://www.sciencedirect.com/journal/medical-image-analysis/publish/guide-for-authors) returned HTTP 403 during the supporting search, so its unverified particulars remain explicit gaps.
- [JGME editors' response-letter guidance](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6476088/): short, sincere thanks rather than repeated exaggerated praise, clear response/change locations and coverage of subquestions. Adapt to the author's reference-based courtesy preference without promising acceptance.
- [K-Dense peer-review](https://github.com/K-Dense-AI/scientific-agent-skills/blob/main/skills/peer-review/SKILL.md): claim-evidence alignment and verification of actual manuscript locations; borrow these principles without converting author responses into reviewer reports or importing an unrelated intake/file pipeline.
