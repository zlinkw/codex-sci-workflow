# Scientific prose and revision principles

Read for actual drafting, substantive revision, polishing or translation of manuscript/reply prose. Pure acceptance, state inspection and editor setup do not load this file. Apply the current kernel's context/evidence gate once. These are the author's standing prose rules, not facts from a previous project.

- Preserve the whole-paper chain **问题/条件 → 挑战 → 设计与真实创新 → 预期与实际证据 → 结论边界**, canonical terms/symbol meanings and author-confirmed structure/reference blueprints. In revisions, reuse the manuscript's established vocabulary and descriptions for the same or similar methods, operations and objects while preserving real distinctions. Do not coin labels, compound terms or acronyms, or rename an existing concept, merely to answer a reviewer or make an explanation sound more novel. Scientific continuity concerns meaning as well as names: ordinary words or an analogy can introduce an unsupported operation, supervision granularity, correspondence, mechanism or capability without creating a new technical term. Ground an explanation in the established definitions, formulations and author-confirmed facts, preserving their objects, levels and relationships. Clarification may unpack those contents but may not silently add a scientific premise; establish a necessary new premise with the author and appropriate support before using it. Apply [terminology continuity](reviewer-response.md#terminology-continuity) when reconciling manuscript and replies. Background serves the problem. Preserve each passage's function as task background, analysis of existing limitations, design motivation or evidence interpretation. Describe module relationships by their established mechanism: co-occurrence alone does not establish complementarity or synergy, so use a neutral relationship description unless the actual roles and evidence support the stronger claim. Do not redefine a field's task around the paper's chosen solution, or replace a valid gap analysis with a generic method description merely to soften an overstatement. Express transitions by actual content relationships, not filler or mechanical sentence patterns. Never invent challenges, necessity, evidence or stronger capabilities.

- Describe the method and concrete implementation facts directly, without empty qualifiers such as In the available implementation or In our implementation. The author's project code is not a partial implementation merely because only part has been inspected. If a material detail remains unclear after checking the relevant source, ask the author a focused question rather than inventing it, silently omitting a necessary answer or narrowing the method to the assistant's current knowledge. Continue independent work while awaiting clarification. Preserve genuine scientific limits and evidence gaps; see [implementation wording and clarification](reviewer-response.md#implementation-wording-and-clarification).

- When a revision changes a concept, method role, rationale, comparison, condition or conclusion strength, trace the same scientific claim throughout the manuscript and its replies, including direct statements, paraphrases and implied inferences. Locate candidates with search, then judge their meaning in context rather than matching keywords alone. Reconcile affected occurrences and dependencies, or retain the specific unresolved ones in the existing state. Distinguish similar terms with different scientific meanings, and retain the claim's relevant variables, assumptions, relationships and outcomes rather than replacing a specific argument with a generic one. Preserve qualifiers, units/aggregation and association versus causation. When the affected Method has declared paper/figure bindings, use the linked drawing module to check those objects and source passages and retain actual staleness in the canonical review state. Ordinary language polishing does not trigger an unrelated whole-paper/figure-library audit or unapproved symbol normalization.

- Favorite references guide Chinese sentence-role/skeleton and length blueprints, not research facts or rigid cloning. For new papers or explicit whole-paper logic work inspect current material, discuss targeted organization choices and obtain that decision before full drafting. Title/abstract blueprint belongs in the top profile; other plans stay local. Distinguish measured reference length, proposed ranges and verified venue limits; Orchestra is an optional seed.

- Substantive argument, not defensive coverage: distinguish objective analysis of existing methods from added disclaimers about the proposed method. In passages that establish a research gap, state the supported limitation, its conditions and consequences directly, and connect it to the corresponding design and mechanism. Preserve informative contrast and purpose constructions such as However, Unlike and To address when their scientific content is justified. Do not append statements about unstudied matters merely to preempt criticism or make the paper appear exhaustive. Do not use the defensive `not to ...` construction in manuscript prose, or replace it with another negative disclaimer serving the same purpose. Each added sentence contributes substantive information needed by the argument. Relevant factual negation, evidence-based limitations and unresolved gaps remain necessary, without turning method paragraphs into repeated disclaimers.

- Revision preference: identify the specific defect that prevents the requested issue from being answered, then change the smallest sufficient span while preserving valid claims, argument depth, mechanism detail and substantive transitions. Read the revised sentence with the complete current paragraph and relevant neighboring sentences, including pending candidates: identify what it carries forward, what new inference it adds and what the following text uses. Read adjacent paragraphs together to understand their roles and handoff, but extend the written changes only where an adjacent premise or transition needs correction for the scoped answer to work. Retain unaffected wording and organization. The one-natural-paragraph review limit is an upper bound, not a default rewriting scope; preparing alternative drafts does not justify enlarging that scope. The revised passage should advance the specific issue or finding carried forward rather than restart the discussion or repeat definitions. Keep each existing paragraph's candidate and ID independent under the review rules. Repeating a general principle after a specific observation, or switching from results to an unexplained method recap, does not establish that relationship. Correct an inaccurate neighboring premise when authorized rather than patching the selected sentence with a connective; ask for a choice when materially different argument structures remain. For a wording correction, keep roughly comparable length and coverage by default; do not compress a rationale or core discussion into a generic sentence merely for brevity. Prefer local rewriting over deletion, retaining the accurate parts when only part of a passage is wrong. Shorten genuine repetition or irrelevant content, without padding to a fixed ratio or imposing a rigid zero-growth quota. Add only information necessary for scientific correctness, reproducibility, evidence or an explicit requirement that existing content cannot accommodate. For manuscript prose, integrate a short clarification into the existing relevant paragraph when it continues the same point, rather than starting a separate one-sentence paragraph or padding it to stand alone. Do not add auxiliary symbols, time indices or parameters merely to explain an existing operation; reuse established notation or plain language unless a genuine scientific distinction requires a new, explicitly defined symbol. For reviewer-driven revisions use [the length tradeoff](reviewer-response.md#revision-length-tradeoff). Preserve approved chapter defaults and coordinate the total budget rather than summing upper bounds. Abstract **180–210 English words, cap 220**; explicit venue requirements take priority. Other module ranges are soft guidance, not targets to fill.

- Manuscript voice: discourage unnecessary first-person wording, not first person itself. Where an established process, module, model or other clear noun accurately names the subject, prefer it to we/our/I. Retain first person when it makes researcher actions, decisions or attribution clearer, or avoids awkward repetition and convoluted sentences. Choose active, concise passive or impersonal clauses according to the actual focus; do not mechanically convert every sentence, assign researcher actions to a model or replace we with the authors. Preserve existing terminology, meaning, tense and evidential strength without padding. This is a subject-selection preference, not a pronoun ban or universal journal rule; see [voice guidance and examples](source-notes.md#manuscript-voice-and-grammatical-subjects). Reply letters retain natural author voice, and verbatim quotations, official names, identifiers and reference titles remain intact. Review affected authored prose without blind whole-project replacement.

- Discuss in Chinese; retain manuscript/reply language/template. Do not use `must` in authored manuscript prose to impose a task requirement. Avoid unsupported absolute claims such as always, necessarily, uniquely or guarantees. Match claim strength to the evidence without uniformly weakening supported criticism or replacing concrete consequences with vague goals. Prior-study descriptions use past tense, current method facts/judgments present, completed experiments appropriate past, completed revisions natural present perfect where useful. Preserve official names/acronyms; avoid uppercase module names except headings/acronyms/fixed names. Avoid unnecessary prose hyphens or underscores, filler, needless colons/parentheses and ambiguous sentence-initial This; necessary prose dashes follow the single-hyphen rule below. No list-like manuscript summaries except Introduction contributions using \item[\textbullet]; replies follow reviewer order.

- New/substantively rewritten ordinary prose is one sentence per source line, no intra-paragraph blank lines and blank lines between paragraphs. Recognize abbreviations/decimals/citations/math; do not mechanically split periods or batch-reflow old prose. Special environments keep suitable structure. After a display equation ending in a period, leave exactly one empty source line before the next prose paragraph; after a comma, leave none and continue the sentence. Apply this after the outer equation environment as detailed in [mathematical notation](mathematical-notation.md). Manual indentation-only formatting preserves source lines and never rewrites/accepts prose or requires Git. Git is optional backup through a separate project-backup action, never part of indentation. Backup commits use a meaningful purpose plus actual file/statistics and author-review boundaries so versions remain searchable.

- Prose punctuation: do not use semicolons (`;` or `；`) in authored manuscript or reply prose, including abstracts, captions and scientific appendices. Use separate sentences or a suitable conjunction while preserving the logical relationship. Do not mechanically replace a semicolon with a comma and create a comma splice. If a dash or hyphen is needed in authored prose, use only one ASCII hyphen `-`, never TeX `--` or `---`, an en dash `–` or an em dash `—`. Prefer words such as to for ranges when clearer. Preserve verbatim reviewer quotations, exact quotations/reference titles, code/CLI/file identifiers and necessary mathematical or LaTeX syntax; this rule does not authorize blind replacement inside them. Check affected authored prose, not a global symbol replacement.
